Five months open. Closed in 22 days.

Nothing about the market changed in between. The brief did.

Abhishek PrasadCo-founder & CBO

Aug 18, 20252 min read

No. 0122days to close, after five months open

A role had been open for five months. Once the brief changed, it closed in twenty-two days. Nothing about the market moved in between.

The search had already seen 327 profiles before we were involved. The team’s explanation was the usual one, and they believed it: the talent isn’t out there. Not at this level, not at this budget, not in this city.

Three hundred and twenty-seven profiles is plenty of talent. What the search was missing was a decision.

Note327 profiles seen. 11 passed over. 2 back in contention. 1 hired.

Where the five months went

Reconstructed afterwards, the delay broke down like this. Six weeks establishing that the job description described a role nobody had written down properly. Seven weeks of interviews in which three panellists were each testing for something slightly different. Three weeks on a candidate everyone liked and nobody would commit to, because no one could say what they were comparing him against.

Very little of that time went on bad candidates. It went on an unresolved conversation, held through candidates, one interview at a time.

Long searches are rarely a sourcing failure. They’re a decision nobody made.
NoteThe intake: 45 minutes, with the hiring manager alone.

What changed in twenty-two days

Forty-five minutes with the hiring manager, alone. What would this person own in month one? What would have to go wrong for it to be a bad hire? From that, six skills, weighted, written down and agreed. Then the same scorecard applied to everyone, including the eleven profiles already in the pipeline that had been passed over.

Two of those eleven came back into contention, and one of them was hired. He had been turned down in month two by a panellist who was, quite reasonably, testing for something the role didn’t need.

The uncomfortable part

Nobody had done anything wrong. The recruiter sourced well, the panel interviewed properly and the hiring manager was engaged throughout. Every step was competent, and the search still took five months, because nobody had said out loud what was being decided.

That’s why we won’t start a search before the intake conversation happens. Of everything in the process, those forty-five minutes are the part that reliably saves months.
