## Findings by Severity

**High**
- **Two different card templates for the same content type.** Pages with 1 post (AI & Assessment, Hiring Philosophy, Hiring Strategy) render a horizontal split card (image left, text right, full-bleed height). The page with 2 posts (Candidate Experience) renders a completely different card shape (image-on-top square, 2-column grid). This isn't just a column-count difference — the aspect ratio and composition of the card itself changes. If a third post is added to any "single-post" category, confirm which template is authoritative; right now the two coexist and would look like two different features if a user browsed categories back-to-back.

**Medium**
- **Eyebrow label breaks the mono/red-caps convention.** "From the journal" (all four category pages) is lowercase, grey, and not monospace — inconsistent with the established pattern of uppercase red mono labels used everywhere else (e.g., "WHAT YOU WILL OWN" on the careers page, "AI & ASSESSMENT" on the post-card metadata directly below it on the same page). Worth aligning to the rest of the system or confirming it's an intentional exception.
- **Thin category pages.** 3 of 4 categories (AI & Assessment, Hiring Philosophy, Hiring Strategy) show only a single article. Not a code bug, but it reads as unfinished/empty next to Candidate Experience's fuller grid — flag to content owner as a coverage gap, not a design blocker.

**Low**
- Numeral formatting "2,00,000" (Indian lakh-style grouping) in the Hiring Strategy headline/dek may read oddly to a non-Indian audience expecting "200,000." Minor, but worth a style-guide decision if the site targets a global audience.

**Claims needing owner evidence (not a design/code issue)**
- "2,00,000 structured interviews across domains" (Hiring Strategy category, headline claim) — large statistical claim, no citation/link visible.
- "Backed by research from McKinsey, Deloitte, and PwC" (Candidate Experience, "Black Hole" article dek) — named third-party research citation with no link or footnote on the listing page; confirm the linked article actually cites sources, since the claim is prominent enough to read as an endorsement.
- Talent Associate JD lists "1 POSITION" — confirm this headcount is still accurate/live before/after any redesign push, since stale headcounts on a live requisition page are a credibility risk.
